     Prompt Redesign: From Rules to Reasoning

     The Core Problem

     The current prompt treats the LLM like a compliance engine:
     - "FIRST SENTENCE PATTERN: [Dimension] [product] in [Finish] adds..."
     - "Target 600-800 characters"
     - "Include ONE natural synonym"
     - "DO NOT mention 28 finishes"

     This is wrong. We're taking away the LLM's ability to THINK about what
     each product needs. The result: descriptions that pass our checklist
     but don't connect with what shoppers actually want to know.

     The question we should be asking: Are descriptions about "attribute
     density for algorithm matching" or about "answering what the buyer
     actually wants to know"?

     ---
     New Philosophy: Context Over Rules

     Instead of telling the LLM WHAT to write, we give it:
     1. Understanding of our customer - who they are, what they're searching
      for, what questions they have
     2. Understanding of our brand - why Allied Brass is worth 4x more than
     Amazon alternatives
     3. Understanding of the goal - make someone click, then buy, then not
     regret it

     Then we TRUST the LLM to figure out what each product needs.

     ## WRITING DESCRIPTIONS

     Before you write, THINK about:

     ### 1. WHO IS SEARCHING FOR THIS?
     - A homeowner renovating a bathroom who wants it to look intentional,
     not afterthought
     - A designer specifying fixtures for a client who expects quality
     - Someone replacing a broken/ugly product who wants an upgrade, not
     just a replacement

     ### 2. WHAT QUESTIONS DO THEY HAVE?
     Every buyer has questions before they spend $80+ on bathroom hardware:
     - "Will this look good in my bathroom?" → They need to visualize it in
     their space
     - "Will this match my other fixtures?" → They need to know about finish
      coordination
     - "Is this actually better than the $20 option?" → They need to
     understand the value
     - "Will this last / is it quality?" → They need trust signals
     (material, warranty)
     - Product-specific questions vary: grab bar buyers ask about weight
     capacity, shower basket buyers ask about drainage

     ### 3. WHAT MAKES US DIFFERENT?
     Allied Brass is NOT competing on price. We're competing on:
     - **Style without sacrifice**: You don't have to choose between "looks
     good" and "works well"
     - **Personalization**: 28 finishes to match any bathroom vision
     - **Innovation**: Rollerless TP holders, retractable rods, decorative
     grab bars, ventilated baskets
     - **Durability**: Solid brass outlasts plastic and die-cast that crack
     and corrode
     - **Coordination**: Match everything across 42+ collections

     ### 4. PLATFORM CONTEXT
     - **Google/Bing (variant)**: One specific finish. This is the
     customer's FIRST impression. Make them want to click.
     - **Shopify (master)**: All finishes on one page. Customer already
     clicked. Help them choose a finish and add to cart.

     ### YOUR TASK
     Write descriptions that answer the buyer's questions and make them
     understand why this product is worth it.

     Don't write for algorithms. Don't stuff keywords. Don't follow
     patterns.
     Write for a human who's about to spend $80 and wants to feel good about
      it.

  Summary: Prompt Redesign - From Rules to Reasoning

  Problem Solved

  The previous prompt approach used prescriptive rules that created robotic,
  templated descriptions:
  - "FIRST SENTENCE PATTERN: [Dimension] [product] in [Finish] adds..."
  - "Target 600-800 characters"
  - "Include ONE natural synonym"

  actually needs.

  New Approach: Context Over Rules

  Instead of telling the LLM WHAT to write, we now give it:
  What We Provide: WHO is searching
  Purpose: Homeowners renovating, designers specifying, people replacing
  products

  What We Provide: WHAT questions buyers have
  Purpose: "Will it look good?", "Will it match?", "Is $80 worth it?"
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  What We Provide: WHAT makes Allied Brass worth it

  ────────────────────────────────────────

  Purpose: Google/Bing = first impression; Shopify = already clicked
